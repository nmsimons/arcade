# L.A.B. look and feel: implementation plan

**Plan for execution, written November 13, 2026. Phases 0 to 4 and 6 are built,
except the steps waiting on measurement; see [Progress](#progress).**

The goal, in the owner's words: a 2D look with a sci-fi edge; the game should
feel awesome; the visuals are meant to be simple; things need to feel real and
grounded.

This plan follows an evaluation of about 60 captured frames (start screen, Wake,
Concourse, six wings, 16 courses, Exit) and of the drawing code. Its findings:

| # | Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Impacts have no visual consequence | The only motion effects are slide grit and foot boosters (`render.ts` `drawMovementEffects`), gravity dust, force-field sparks and EMP arcs. A landing draws nothing. Entering water moves the surface a few pixels and draws nothing else |
| 2 | In daylight the robot is the faintest thing on screen | Contrast against the wall `#f1f1ed`: robot shell `#b5bdb2` 1.70:1, limbs `#a9b7ad` 1.84:1, stone 2.44:1, shovebot 3.46:1, graffiti 5.92:1 |
| 3 | Actors and props look pasted on | Terrain and mechanisms cast daylight and lamp shadows on the wall; the player, crates, balls and robots cast none in play. Lamps, clocks and thin slabs have no visible mounting |
| 4 | Large terrain masses show a wallpaper pattern; water reads as tinted glass | The grain is an 80×40 tile with 2 to 4 marks (`terrainMaterials.ts`). Water is one flat 35% tint with a waterline at 1.3 px and 60% alpha |
| 5 | The rooms carry no sci-fi of their own; wings look alike | Wing levels are stub corridors on the same wall. The facility spec already says wing looks need new art |
| 6 | Small off-brand moments | The loading card uses the paper theme a second before the phosphor start screen |

Night and spotlit scenes (Wake, Spelunk, Quarantine III, the Freight wing)
already meet the goal. Do not restyle them.

Two corrections to the evaluation as first reported: a waterline highlight does
exist (it is faint, not absent), and changing the wall colour is dropped,
because 34 test files locate the camera by the wall's `#f1f1ed` fill.

## Rules for every phase

1. **No simulation changes.** Golden traces hash physics, state and poses, and
   they can only be checked on the owner's Apple Silicon Mac. Do not edit the
   stepping code in `model.ts`, `challenge.ts`, `playerContacts.ts`,
   `propPhysics.ts`, `climbables.ts`, `gravity.ts`, `robotPhysics.ts`,
   `repulsorBot.ts`, or the advance functions in `waterSurface.ts`. Do not add
   fields to `Run`, `Player` or `Player.anim`. New presentation state lives
   outside them (see phase 1).
2. **Both renderers draw it.** Artwork is written once against the Canvas 2D
   API; WebGL records the same calls (`webgl/recorder.ts`) and declines a frame
   it cannot reproduce, which then falls back to Canvas. Use flat fills, strokes
   and paths. After each phase, open two daylight courses and one night course
   in Edge with a GPU and confirm `window.jumpingWorldFrame.declined` stays
   `null` and `declines` gains no new reason.
3. **Lighting contract.** Every world draw goes through
   `paint(ctx, exposure, draw, receivesLight, bounds)` (`worldPaint.ts`). New
   effects receive light (exposure 0). Do not add glow, blur, gradients or
   emission.
4. **Evaluated, never simulated.** Follow `drawGravityDust`
   (`gravityRender.ts:111`): positions come from a hash and elapsed gameplay
   time (`run.activeTime`), with a fixed upper bound on shapes, nothing drawn
   off screen, no `Math.random`, no wall-clock time, and no allocation per
   frame beyond the event list. Pausing freezes every effect.
5. **Performance.** Before a phase, and again after it, run
   `node scripts/profile-jumping-level.mjs <level> --bundle before=<dir> --bundle after=<dir>`
   so the episodes interleave, on `LAB.jump-level.json`,
   `Tower.jump-level.json`, `Quarantine II.jump-level.json` and
   `exit.jump-level.json`, at DPR 1 and 1.5. A phase passes when median frame
   CPU and frame rate are within run-to-run noise of the baseline. Record the
   figures in `docs/jumping-performance.md`, as earlier work did.
6. **Tests.** `npm run test:fast`, `npm test`, `npm run lint`, `npm run build`,
   then the affected browser specs, including the WebGL projects
   (`$env:JUMPING_WEBGL = '1'; npx playwright test <spec>`). When a phase
   replaces tested behaviour, change that test on purpose and name it in the
   phase notes; never loosen an assertion to make it pass. Add a render unit
   test with a recording context for each new draw function (the pattern is in
   `tests/jumping-water-surface.test.mjs:121`). A saved screenshot is not a
   test (`docs/testing.md`).
7. **Docs move with the code.** When a phase changes a contract in
   `docs/jumping-lighting.md`, `docs/jumping-performance.md` or
   `docs/jumping-levels.md`, update that text in the same change.
8. **The working tree is not clean.** At the time of writing, 18 tracked files
   have uncommitted changes (among them `render.ts`, `challengeRender.ts`,
   `goal.ts`, `level.ts` and `UntitledJumpingGame.tsx`), and
   `public/levels/jumping/Untitled level.jump-level.json` is untracked. Build
   on those changes; do not revert, reformat or stage them. Do not edit level
   JSON except where a phase says so, and then follow `AGENTS.md`.
9. **Git.** Work on `main`. Commit or push only when the owner asks. Keep each
   phase, and the items marked "own change", separately revertable.
10. **Review sheets.** Each phase ends with before and after captures of the
    same scenes in `.local/look/<phase>/` (ignored by Git) for the owner to
    look at. Report what was verified and what was not.

## Phase 0: baseline and tooling

1. Add `scripts/capture-lab-look.mjs`, which captures a fixed list of scenes
   from a running dev or preview server into a folder. What makes it work:
   - Launch Playwright's Chromium with `channel: 'msedge'` and `--enable-gpu`.
     Headless Chromium has software WebGL, which the game refuses.
   - Unlock courses by seeding `localStorage`: `arcade.jumping.completed.v1` as
     `{ <level id>: true }` and `arcade.jumping.times.v1` as `{ <level id>: 0 }`
     for every file in `public/levels/jumping/index.json`. Open
     `/lab/levels/built-in/<file>` and wait for `.jumping-state`.
   - Hubs do not open by link. Set `arcade.jumping.facility.v1` to
     `{ place: { kind: 'hub', level, arrival: { kind: 'door', id } } }` and open
     `/lab/facility`.
   - To start elsewhere in a course, intercept the POST to
     `**/__arcade/jumping-levels` (response `files[].{fileName, level, sourceText}`)
     and move `entrance` in both `level` and `sourceText`. The spot must pass
     `entranceFits` (`goal.ts`), or the level is rejected.
   - Use the fake clock as the browser specs do (`page.clock`, helpers in
     `tests/browser/helpers/`), so the same scene gives the same frame.
2. Scenes, each at 1600×900: the start screen; Wake; Concourse at
   `concourse-to-opening`; Freight at `door-jk`; Security at `door-lock-out-i`;
   the courses `LAB`, `Tower`, `scaffold1`, `Waterline2`, `Quarantine II`,
   `Quarantine III`, `lockout1`, `world1`, `fulltilt`, `buoyancy`; `spelunk`
   from `{ x: 3000, y: 1780 }`; `exit` from `{ x: 20300, y: 2320 }` and
   `{ x: 16760, y: 2445 }`. Add three short frame sequences: a running jump and
   landing in Tower, a fall into the first pool in LAB, and a crate dropped off
   a ledge.
3. Capture `.local/look/baseline/` and record the performance baseline (rule 5).

## Phase 1: impact feedback

The largest gap against "feels awesome". Sound already knows about every impact:
`JumpingAudioState.step` (`audioState.ts`) detects player and robot landings with
their fall speed, box and ball impacts with a strength, and water entries with
a strength and size, each with a world position.

1. **Effect events.** Add `worldEffects.ts`: a small fixed-capacity list of
   `{ kind, x, y, strength, size, angle, time }`, where `time` is
   `run.activeTime` at the event. Feed it from the same observations that make
   the sound cues, at the point where `cue(kind, x, y, strength, size)` is
   called, so sound and picture always agree. Keep it off `Run`: hold it in a
   `WeakMap<Run, WorldEffects>` or pass it beside the run, whichever fits the
   call sites better. It resets with `audioState.reset`. Studio previews and
   thumbnails have no events and draw nothing.
2. **Landing dust** (`drawImpactEffects` in a new `impactRender.ts`, called in
   `drawPuzzleWorld` next to `drawMovementEffects`, before the athlete). On a
   player or walking-robot landing above a moderate fall speed, two low puffs
   travel outward along the ground angle: 5 to 7 flat specks each, 0.35 s, at
   most 10 units of rise, alpha no higher than 0.5, size and reach scaled by
   strength. Use the slide grit's drawing style and colour (`render.ts:136`).
   A light landing draws nothing.
3. **Prop impacts.** For `box-impact` and `ball-impact` events: a few short
   ticks at the contact point, 0.25 s, scaled by strength and prop size.
4. **Water entry** (D1). At the entry point on the waterline: two flat lobes
   that rise 8 to 28 units by strength and fall back, and 6 to 10 droplets on
   ballistic arcs computed in closed form, gone within 0.6 s, in the waterline
   colour family. Draw it after the water tint, clipped to above the surface.
   Draw it from the new module, so `drawWaterSurfaceDetails` still emits no
   `fillRect` and its unit test stands. Draw nothing when the water surface is
   disabled (`run.waterSurface.enabled === false`, the reduced-quality mode).
   Then read `tests/browser/jumpingWaterPerformance*.spec.mjs` and its helper,
   and change the droplet expectations for normal quality on purpose.
5. **Camera settle** (D4, own change, last). On a hard player landing only,
   offset the view down by 3 to 5 screen pixels, decaying over 0.18 s. Apply it
   where the camera is assembled in `paint` (`UntitledJumpingGame.tsx:954`), or
   inside `GameCamera`, never in simulation state. Skip it when
   `prefers-reduced-motion` is set; the game has no such check in its world
   drawing today, so add one with `matchMedia`. Run `jumpingCamera*.spec.mjs`
   and `jumpingCameraEncounters.spec.mjs`.
6. Update `docs/jumping-lighting.md` section 4 with the new effects' lighting
   rule (they receive light, cast nothing, emit nothing).

Done when: the three frame sequences show dust, ticks and a splash; a walk and
a small hop show none; effects freeze on pause and vanish on restart; the
performance check passes; WebGL declines nothing.

## Phase 3: grounding

1. **Object shadows in play** (D2). Shadows for the player, props and robots
   already exist as `shadows: 'full'` (`lightingModel.ts` `dynamicCasters`,
   `athleteShadow.ts`); play uses `'structural'`. Measure `'full'` on the WebGL
   renderer on Tower, Boxes, Quarantine II and LAB (rule 5). If it passes, pass
   `'full'` from the gameplay render calls in `UntitledJumpingGame.tsx` and
   keep `'structural'` under the adaptive reduction. If it fails, try the
   player alone, then daylight alone, and report the figures rather than
   shipping a regression. Update the sentence in `docs/jumping-performance.md`
   and section 5 of `docs/jumping-lighting.md` to match what ships.
2. **Mounts.** Give lamps a short wall plate or arm (`lightFixture.ts`), wall
   clocks and coin displays two small fixings (`digitalDisplay.ts` housing),
   and the goal lamp pole a base (`challengeRender.ts` `drawGoal`). Flat shapes
   in the existing equipment greens, inside each object's current paint bounds
   where possible; widen the declared bounds where not.
3. **Slab brackets** (D5, comparison sheet only). On the wall layer, behind
   terrain: a small bracket near each end of any terrain piece that is thin
   (24 units or less), roughly level, and touches no other terrain. Compute
   once per level and cache as `relief` does (`surfaceDepth.ts:69`). Capture
   scaffold1, world1, Quarantine II and Tower with and without, and stop there.

## Phase 4: materials

1. **Grain.** Replace each material's tile with a larger one (about 240×160
   world units) holding 8 to 12 marks at irregular positions, same colours and
   mark sizes, so no row or column of dashes lines up across a big mass. Check
   how WebGL uploads the pattern tile before changing its size. Chalk stays
   plain. Materials are appearance only; friction must not change.
2. **Water.** Raise the waterline to full alpha at about 2 px, and add one
   darker flat band below a fixed depth (two tones in total, no gradient).
   `#58a9df` appears in five test files and `#bce3f2` in one: keep those colour
   values for the existing shapes and add the band as a new draw.
3. Review sheets: buoyancy, LAB, Waterline2, Quarantine II, exit.

## Phase 5: wall finishes and wing identity (D6, not started without a go-ahead)

Outline only, for a later decision. `environment.walls[].kind` is
`'facility' | 'cave'` (`environment.ts`). A wing look would add a wall finish
choice with two or three variants drawn in `drawCourseWall` and
`drawWallPanels` (panel joints with fixings and no fine grid; tiled for Water;
dark bulkhead for Security), an editor control, the schema text in
`docs/jumping-levels.md`, and one field in each wing's hub file under the
`AGENTS.md` process. It should be planned with the wing layout work in
`docs/jumping-facility.md`, which is still open.

## Phase 6: small polish

1. **Loading card.** Give "Opening the facility." and "The facility could not
   open." (`UntitledJumpingGame.tsx:1133`) the phosphor system-display styling
   the start and pause screens use. Check `menuThemes.spec.mjs`,
   `jumpingMessageScreen.dev.spec.mjs` and `jumpingInterface.dev.spec.mjs`
   first, since they cover these dialogs at several sizes.
2. **Label face.** In one capture the "Quarantine Field" labels appeared in a
   plain sans rather than the stencil face. Find out whether that is a
   font-loading race (`useWallTextFont.ts`) or the capture's timing. Fix it only
   if it reproduces in normal play.
3. **Resolution cap.** `lightingPixelRatio` (`lightingBudget.ts`) caps the world
   at two million pixels, which is about 1× on a 4K display at 200% scaling.
   Measure a higher cap for daylight levels without lamps or region lighting on
   WebGL. Raise it only if rule 5 passes at DPR 2; otherwise record the figures
   and leave it.
4. **Clouds.** Redraw the three cloud contours in `environment.ts` as longer,
   flatter shapes. Same colour, count, scroll rate and baseline.

## Not in this plan

- The camera zoom and the robot's size on screen. They change how much of a
  route is visible in a timed game.
- Structures or a horizon in Exit, and the bus's head-on drawing. These are
  level authoring and art direction under D8 and `AGENTS.md`.
- The stone-coloured surround outside small rooms. No clear fix yet.

## Order and stopping points

Phases 1 to 3 are the core and are independent of each other; do them in that
order. Phases 4 and 6 are smaller and can follow in either order. Stop and
report, rather than work around it, if: a phase needs a simulation change; a
performance check fails after the fallbacks listed; WebGL declines a frame
because of new artwork and the artwork cannot be redrawn within what the
recorder supports; or a test encodes a decision this plan did not list.
