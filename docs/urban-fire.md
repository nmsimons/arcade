# Urban Fire

The playable district is fixed at 1,600 × 1,100 game units, inside a larger rendered city. The camera stays centered on the jeep, including at the perimeter. The default view uses 0.8× scale at the 1,280 × 800 reference size, showing 25% more world in each dimension. Larger displays scale proportionally; smaller displays retain 0.8×. Resizing only changes the view. There is no minimap; the HUD keeps armor, wave, score, and hostile counts around the unobstructed battlefield.

Arrow keys or WASD steer, accelerate, and reverse. Changing the jeep's heading requires actual forward or backward travel: it cannot pivot when parked or blocked against cover. Steering builds gradually with speed, remains available while coasting, and reverses yaw naturally while backing up. The front wheels follow steering input even while stopped, smoothly turning up to about 35 degrees in either direction and returning to center when released; controller input gives proportional wheel angles. Space fires, with no limit on simultaneous jeep projectiles. P pauses and Escape exits. Standard controllers use the left stick to steer, RT/R2 to drive forward, LT/L2 to reverse, A/× to fire, and Menu/Options to pause. Menus use the same focus and controller navigation as Hard Vacuum. Inputs must be released after entering gameplay, and losing focus or disconnecting pauses combat.

Vehicles use solid matte faces, beveled geometry, directional light, and soft grounded shadows. Urban Fire selects solid materials from the shared model renderer; the other games retain their existing rendering. The light stays fixed in world space as vehicles turn. The jeep is a compact scout with a broad squared hood, one blue windshield, a simple olive cabin, four chunky rolling tires, and a single rear spare. Its pale body, dark tires, and quiet surfaces give it a recognizable silhouette with few small details. Front-wheel steering and gently easing suspension remain. Copper tanks have broad 10-unit tread belts with 7-unit rolling bands (including opposing motion during pivots), independently rotating raised turrets, coral firing indicators, and recoiling barrels. Brass helicopters bank into their flight path, pitch with forward motion, and carry projected spinning rotors, glass canopies, skids, and displaced ground shadows. Muzzle flashes are attached to the projected barrels. Damage darkens armor and adds fine breaches and flickering instruments. The jeep emits no damage smoke above two remaining armor. At two, light intermittent puffs emerge from its hood; at one, a much darker, larger, denser plume signals critical condition. Puffs inherit some vehicle motion, then drift and rise independently. Repair above two stops emission and lets existing smoke disperse. Tanks retain their small hot fragments. Body wear reflects damage below the base three armor points. Animation uses a separate simulation-driven clock and freezes during pause; it does not change collision footprints, aiming, damage, or movement tuning.

Repair pickups are small physical medical cases: pale beveled lids, a painted red cross, olive bases, latches, a handle, and grounded shadows. Armor caches are stacks of blue-gray steel plates strapped to low crates, with a pale painted shield. The jeep starts with three armor points. Medical cases repair only to three and remain unused at three or above; they never replace lost extra armor. An armor cache first repairs anything below three to three, then adds one point to the current total. There is no fixed stacking cap. Each wave supplies at most one new medical case and one armor cache, with an uncollected item carrying over instead of accumulating duplicates. Both descend beneath small round parachutes, touch down in a little dust, and become collectible only after landing. Pickups require proximity and a clear path through cover. The HUD shows current armor, with additional blue pips for extra protection and an exact numeric total. There are no pickup pop-up messages. Redeploy resets armor to three.

Projectiles have shaded metal bodies, bright noses, and short soft motion smears; enemy shells are slightly broader and warmer. Muzzle flashes are brief filled bursts followed by fading smoke. Destruction produces compact fire, dust, and rising smoke instead of expanding outline rings. Concrete impacts shed dusty chips; metal impacts add short-lived hot fragments. Fragments tumble, fall, bounce, settle, and fade, with local deterministic variation that does not consume combat randomness. All effects freeze during pause. Projectile speed, damage, collision positions, and the lack of a player projectile cap are unchanged.

Four avenues and three cross streets define 20 city blocks. Buildings occupy actual parcels with continuous sidewalk setbacks and entrances facing streets or connected service drives. The blocks have different uses: deep freight depots and loading aprons, smaller residential parcels around shared courts and lawns, offices with parking, utility yards, and a civic exchange with four paved forecourts. A neighborhood green and vacant demolition lots provide open maneuvering space. Five bombed-out buildings have exposed floors, broken perimeter walls, and large rubble slabs; the surviving pieces are solid cover, while the interiors are accessible to the jeep.

Three 38-unit through alleys connect adjacent streets. They fit the jeep's 24-unit collision diameter but exclude tank hulls, offering shortcuts and escape routes; tanks can navigate around them via the avenues. All 14 road exits are obstructed by continuous Jersey barriers topped with concertina razor wire. Reinforcements stage inside these closures, at least 450 units from the jeep. The player starts on the southern street facing the workshop alley. Roads, buildings, ruins, lots, alleys, closures, spawns, and patrol destinations share one layout definition.

Tanks arrive in staggered parachute drops. Red smoke marks clear street landing sites, followed by a 2.6-second descent under round cargo canopies viewed from overhead. Shaded fabric panels, suspension lines, elevated hulls, and displaced ground shadows show the height. Touchdown produces dust, a brief suspension bounce, and a low thump; the canopy slackens and disperses. Tanks can be hit once grounded, but wait another 0.8 seconds before driving and fighting. If traffic enters a landing site, the suspended tank glides along clear pavement rather than landing on the jeep or a parked car. Helicopters start at least 180 world units beyond both the current viewport and the district, including ultrawide views and perimeter camera positions, then fly continuously toward an entry point without a boundary clamp. Incoming enemies count toward the current wave and are indicated separately on the HUD. All arrival clocks freeze during pause.

The city continues 1,024 units beyond the playable district in every direction, with extended streets, neighboring blocks, a northern freight railway, industrial yards, and a southern river. Two bridge spans are broken and two intact bridges are barricaded; streets continue on the far bank. There is no continuous perimeter wall or decorative map frame. Existing outer buildings extend away from their original street facades into the surrounding city. Their entrance positions remain fixed. Only gaps beside open parcels receive additional service annexes, and smaller openings or exposed ruins are blocked by irregular piles of masonry. These visible structures stop the jeep before the fallback map clamp. The surrounding scenery is cached with the city.

The city reads as a miniature battlefield under soft northwest light. Solid matte surfaces, warm concrete, cool asphalt, earth-toned roofs, mossy greens, directional shading, and soft cast shadows replace the luminous outlines. Dark contact shadows anchor hard cover; flat open ground has no raised border. Material gradients remain broad and quiet. Connected rubble reads as one chipped silhouette, with large fractured concrete panels, subordinate stones, and bent reinforcing rods. Roofless buildings retain broken masonry caps and a small section of surviving roof; these remnants participate in collision, while soot, floor scars, and scattered ground chips remain traversable. Jersey barriers have broad bases, sloping shoulders, lifting sockets, and inset amber warning panels. Overlapping razor-wire coils and their small blades sit along the crowns; the whole closure is solid cover. Thicker stop lines and approach chevrons give the closures a consistent traffic treatment.

The freight corridor has supported rails, crossing panels, signals, and composed trains with diesel locomotives, boxcars, tank wagons, container flatcars, and loaded gondolas. Bogies, couplers, cab glass, and separate body planes establish their scale. The river has quiet current marks, quay coping and mooring fixtures; bridges have visible piers and railings, while missing spans expose fractured deck edges, girders, reinforcing rods, and submerged slabs. These structures remain subdued enough to keep the playable street network and vehicles legible.

The playable neighborhoods show interrupted civilian use. The freight yard has an ambushed delivery truck and municipal bus; the civic exchange has an abandoned aid post with tents, a medical truck, supplies, and sandbags. Ordinary cars remain in parking bays and against curbs. The residential green retains benches and dense planted trees beside a shell scar, while apartment shopfronts have closed shutters and folded awnings. The eastern industrial area carries the heavier damage: burned cargo, street impacts, fallen lamps, broken curbs, and localized roof breaches. Dust fans extend from actual ruined entrances, and skid marks connect abandoned vehicles to their approaches. These scenes are authored around real parcels instead of scattered randomly.

Trees, aid-post equipment, intact benches, standing ruin walls, surviving roofs, and perimeter rubble remain full obstacles. Trees stay solid regardless of condition. Burned vehicles, splintered benches, loose interior rubble, and shell craters are passable rough terrain: they slow the jeep and tanks, with tracks retaining more traction. Four ground contacts drive chassis pitch, roll, wheel travel, and damped vertical suspension; crater bowls dip the body while wreckage lifts it. Stationary vehicles settle rather than vibrating forever. Loose wreckage has low relief and short contact shadows; intact cover has taller shaded faces and longer cast shadows. Soot, dropped papers, tire marks, and fallen lamp pieces remain flat. Curbside cars initially leave a continuous 60-unit tank corridor; every lot remains reachable by the jeep and keeps more than three quarters of its area open. Three wrecks shed faint drifting smoke and small embers on the presentation clock; these freeze during pause and render beneath vehicles and projectiles.

The six intact civilian vehicles are independent heavy bodies with rounded rolling tires, separate cabs and glass, contact-driven rotation, and sprung chassis motion. Jeep and tank collisions exchange momentum; shots shift the nearest exposed car and off-center hits impart torque. Tires resist sideways scrubbing more than rolling and quickly settle. Cars collide with each other, permanent cover, trees, roadblocks, and battlefield bounds. Their rotated collision shapes, projectile cover, and visibility checks follow their current poses. Permanent structures define tank routes; tanks can push cars out of their way. Redeploy resets parked vehicles, and pause freezes their physics and presentation. Static scenery stays cached while moving vehicles render separately.

Jeep collisions sound according to the struck material: dry concrete thuds for buildings, rubble, and Jersey barriers; crumpled sheet metal for civilian vehicles; lower, heavier armored clanks for tanks; hollow knocks for trees, benches, and crates; and muted impacts for tents and sandbags. Loudness follows relative speed into the contact surface before the collision response, so glancing touches stay lighter and moving vehicles can audibly ram a stationary jeep. Solver substeps and adjacent faces merge into one impact. Sustained pushing stays quiet; separation or a new sharp jolt rearms the contact. Impact samples use local deterministic variation and do not affect physics or combat randomness. Pause, death, and exit quickly fade active collision sounds. Run `node --test tests/urbanFireCollisions.test.mjs` for contact direction and relative velocity, repeat suppression, rearming, material selection, unchanged civilian physics, and distinct bounded sound samples.

Hard jeep corners produce a gritty tire squeal and two subtle rear-tire tracks on pavement. Volume and mark strength follow actual sideways slip and travel after collision resolution; parked steering, blocked throttle, gentle turns, grass, and rough debris remain quiet. Thin, translucent marks stay fixed on the ground and fade completely after 3.5 simulation seconds. The selected sound uses a 1.5 kHz tone with 4.5 rasp, smoothed random frequency targets spanning ±490 Hz at an irregular rate around 80 changes per second, and a separate broadband noise layer at 20% gain. The tone filter centers on 4.6 kHz with a broad 0.3 Q; overall volume remains at 100%. Random target intervals avoid a repeating vibrato. The noise bypasses the tone filter. The four-second two-layer buffer is cached. Sound parameters live in `tireSound.ts`. Pausing freezes mark age and silences both layers; resuming restores them only while sliding. Tire effects do not change handling or consume combat randomness.
